What we would build

Six systems on one platform — each answers a problem the operation already has. One is working software today and can be demonstrated at the table; the rest are designed and sequenced on page four.

01

LP Onboarding Rails

THE PROBLEM — Fund I’s LP paperwork runs through a fund administrator and email: subscription packets and accreditation letters collected by hand, capital calls issued as one-off PDFs, the AML/KYC procedure still marked “TBD” on the fund’s own document checklist — and a signed side letter owes co-invest offers (two a year, four per investment period) that no system anywhere is tracking.

A fund-onboarding rail inside the Operating System: invite → accreditation questionnaire → document upload → verified → countersigned → funded — each step stamped, dated, and visible from one desk. Capital calls and side-letter obligations live on the same ledger and surface themselves before they come due, not after.

OUTCOME — A new fund onboards in a day. The incoming corporate partnership (up to $250K per deal, roughly ten deals over twelve months) and Fund II’s November first close board the same rail — and the $22–55K-a-year fund-administration line stops growing.

02

The Events Desk WORKING SOFTWARE · DEMO TODAY

THE PROBLEM — The spring Invitational — 175 guests, a $34K night at 35% margin — ran on a Google Form, a master spreadsheet with hand-typed Approved / Player / Waitlist columns, a second response tracker built after the fact, and sponsor deliverables buried inside PDF agreements.

One events desk: a public RSVP page → an approval queue → phone-first check-in → foursome pairings → a live scorecard → sponsor deliverables tracked item by item ($20,000 title slot, three $8,000 partner slots). Built for the fall Golf Party; reusable for every event after it, including the Q4 launch event.

OUTCOME — Pick the date and RSVPs open the same day. This one already exists — we’ll walk through it together at the table.

03

The Talent Wire

THE PROBLEM — A 500-person vetted talent network sits in a spreadsheet while the operating plan budgets $8K rising to $55K a year for recruiting tools — and the placement arm is set to launch with a commission pool but no product underneath it.

A swipe-based matching wire for CPG talent: operators on one side, roles on the other, curated the way the paper curates everything. Matching is single-sided by design — one swipe from either side starts the introduction; no mutual-match wait.

OUTCOME — The network becomes the placement arm’s engine: top-of-funnel for retained searches, powered by people who are already vouched for.

04

The Reader

THE PROBLEM — Roughly 3,800 subscribers read the paper by email alone — 97,108 papers delivered across 83 sends, engagement four times the industry benchmark — but there is no product to open in the morning and nothing to share except a forward, while the founder’s LinkedIn does a million views a year on its own.

A newspaper-styled reader app: the daily CPG front page (the top-15 wire already runs on the platform), DEALS episodes, and the JOBS board. Every article ships as a clipping — a designed, shareable cut made for LinkedIn and iMessage.

OUTCOME — Circulation becomes a product with a growth loop built in — and the audience number every sponsorship rate is priced on starts compounding.

05

The Sponsorship Desk

THE PROBLEM — H2 carries a six-figure sellable inventory — the Golf Party, the Summit, JOBS and DEALS placements, platform partnerships — managed in one spreadsheet, sold conversation by conversation, with every deliverable promised in a PDF and tracked from memory.

A sponsorship desk on the platform: the inventory board with open and sold slots, a sponsor pipeline from prospect to delivered, per-sponsor deliverable checklists — and a gated media kit, where the circulation numbers do the selling from behind an email gate.

OUTCOME — Sponsors self-serve into the pipeline, every promise is tracked to delivery, and inventory stops leaking between conversations.

06

The Quiet Growth Pages

THE PROBLEM — The public site converts attention into nothing structured: brand applications land in a form-response sheet, site traffic has grown +367% year over year with 45% arriving direct — and there is still no path from that attention to an LP conversation or a filed deal.

Two unlisted pages and one wire: an invest page built 506(c)-clean — accreditation self-certification, then the data room and a call — shared personally and never placed in the navigation; and founder application 2.0, which files itself into the deal pipeline, deck and all, the moment it is submitted.

OUTCOME — Growth stops depending on memory. Every inbound founder, reader, and prospective LP lands somewhere that compounds.

Already in place, as of this week —  the pipeline of record (every deal, priorities restored, one board), brand files attached to every deal page, the whole operation readable from the founder’s existing Claude subscription through the platform connector, site applications wired straight into the pipeline, and the events desk itself. The foundations the six systems above stand on.
